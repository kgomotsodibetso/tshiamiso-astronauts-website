On 29 May, 40 learners sat in the computer room at Khunoana Primary School and waited for their turn to spell. It was the first qualifier of the 2026 TA Regional Spelling Bee.

Four months later, nine schools have held qualifiers, more than 320 learners have spelled, and 107 of them have earned a place at the Grand Final on Friday 23 October. This is how we got here.

## Where it started

In October 2024 we ran a spelling bee at Chief Bambatha Primary School. Forty learners from Grades 4 to 7 took part. Last year we grew it into a regional event. This year we took it to nine schools across the Sedibeng West District (D8), with a qualifier at each one.

## Getting ready

On 29 April we trained our moderators and the teacher liaisons from the participating schools in a three-hour workshop, with the district's endorsement. About two weeks before its qualifier, each school received a "Spelling Bee in a Box": official word lists in three levels, a sealed tie-breaker envelope, a participation register, role cards and a report template.

The rules were the same everywhere:

- One incorrect letter ends a learner's turn.
- A learner may restart a word, but letters already spoken cannot be taken back.
- Only the school's teacher liaison can lodge an appeal, and only about pronunciation or a broken rule.
- Two adults are always present.
- We take photographs only of learners whose parents have signed a consent form.

The rooms changed: a computer room, school halls, a classroom, a staff room and a library. The routine did not. The pronouncer read the word, the learner repeated it, spelled it letter by letter and repeated it again, and the scorekeeper marked it.

## What our moderators saw

We read the summary reports our moderators filed. A few things stood out.

Most reports use the same words: nervous, excited, prepared. Evaton Primary had our biggest qualifier, with 46 learners, and its moderators noted that the learners were confident. At Setlabotjha, Grade 4 and 5 learners were well prepared, while fewer Grade 6 and 7 learners put their hands up. At Khunoana, the words were harder for Grades 5 to 7, and we ended with 11 finalists instead of 12.

Ekujuleni needed the tie-breaker envelope. Most schools did not. A few schools had appeals, and each time the learner was given a chance to spell the word again.

We also learned where to push next. At Emanzini, the moderators wrote that learners need more practice with the rules. At Evaton West, they wrote that learners need mentoring and practice. Nozipho Mazamisa, our Head of Literacy, moderated every qualifier we have reports for.
